# Activity 3 — Reflection draft for Nidhish (review and customise)

These are draft ideas based on the final website. Write your own account of which choices you made and challenges you actually experienced before submitting.

**1. Purpose:** My website aims to help teenagers recognise cyberbullying, choose safer ways to respond, and understand where to get support. I wanted the message that pausing before acting can change what happens next.

**2. Target audience:** Students and teenagers. I used short headlines, familiar examples involving group chats and reposting, an interactive graph, diagrams and a modern dark theme to keep the topic relevant.

**3. Proud of:** The chapter-by-chapter experience. The portal navigation connects the five lessons, and each chapter lets the user take a small action rather than only reading information.

**4. Colour/layout/fonts:** Dark colours give the site a serious tone while the warm orange accent represents attention and hope. The large editorial headings build emphasis, and clear diagrams explain practical steps.

**5. Multimodal modes:** Written, visual, spatial, gestural and aural modes. These include photography, generated artwork, interactive charts and diagrams, motion, navigation, opt-in sound and spoken narration.

**6. Strongest mode:** The visual and interactive modes work together because visitors can see how cyberbullying patterns develop and then select different responses.

**7. Words and images:** The written content explains what the lesson means, while images and diagrams make the situation easier to imagine. Removing either would make the message less effective.

**8. Removing a mode:** Without the diagrams, the site would become more like a normal article; it would lose opportunities for users to explore safer choices.

**9. Engagement:** Visitors can switch between data views, explore fictional situations, and write private reflections. This makes them participate rather than simply scroll.

**10. Challenges:** Explain honestly any issues you noticed when reviewing the deployed site. Possible areas include making the mobile design readable, keeping animations smooth, and ensuring controls work with the keyboard.

**11. Three future improvements:** After proper testing, possible improvements include more accessible translations, a reviewed set of real anonymous stories with consent, and more evidence-based interactive scenarios. A truly public comment service would need moderation and privacy safeguards first.
